Section 4 — Problem Mirror (6 Sourcing Challenges)
Headline: Sourcing from India works — when you have the right partner.
Jaipur and its surrounding clusters produce some of the world&apos;s best handcrafted goods. The karigar traditions in silver and stone-setting, the block-printing villages of Sanganer and Bagru, the wood-carving workshops of Jodhpur, the marble inlay artisans — none of this is replicated elsewhere at India&apos;s price point. The challenge isn&apos;t the region. It&apos;s that the supply ecosystem is large, fragmented, and built for buyers who already have relationships.
If you don&apos;t have those relationships yet, six things tend to go wrong. Each is solvable — but only if you know what to look for.
1. Timelines that slip without warning
&apos;Two weeks&apos; becomes four. Four becomes eight. Updates get vague, then sparse. Sample and production timelines are often quoted optimistically — workshops are juggling multiple orders, and yours can quietly slide down the priority list when a bigger client walks in.
What we do: agreed timelines with weekly check-ins, and direct visibility into workshop workload before you commit.
2. Samples that drift from the reference
You send a moodboard, a tech pack, exact specs. The sample comes back close — but not quite. Slightly different proportions. A finish that&apos;s almost but not exactly what you asked for. The cause is usually translation, not deception: tech packs read differently to a karigar than they do to you, and workshops naturally default toward what they already know how to make.
What we do: we walk through your references with the karigar in person before sampling starts, so what comes out is what you actually asked for.
3. Quality that varies between sample and bulk
The sample looks great. The bulk run, made by different hands across multiple days, doesn&apos;t match it as tightly. Finish consistency, dimensional accuracy, color matching, material grade — these are the variables that quietly drift between the first 50 pieces and the next 500.
What we do: pre-shipment inspection on every order — physical comparison against the approved sample, defect checks, and count verification before goods leave the workshop.
4. The first-time-buyer pricing gap
Workshops quote new buyers higher prices and firmer MOQs than they quote repeat customers. It&apos;s not malicious — it&apos;s how relationship-driven economies work everywhere. But if you&apos;re new, you can pay 15-25% more than you should, and accept MOQs that are negotiable for someone with leverage.
What we do: we negotiate as a portfolio buyer. Our clients get prices and MOQs that wouldn&apos;t be offered to a stranger walking in cold.
5. Telling a real workshop from a middleman
Many &apos;manufacturers&apos; you find online are actually trading companies — they take your order and subcontract it to whichever workshop has capacity. You pay the middleman markup and have no idea who&apos;s actually making your pieces. The work can still be fine, but you&apos;ve lost the ability to control quality or build a real relationship.
What we do: every workshop in our network has been physically visited. We&apos;ve seen the production floor, met the karigars, audited the capability. You work with the actual maker.
6. Communication that fragments across channels
WhatsApp at 2 AM your time. A different person every week. Updates split across emails, voice notes, and three group chats. By month two you&apos;re spending more time chasing answers than running your brand.
What we do: one direct contact, one channel, one source of truth. Weekly photo updates during active production, without you asking.
Closing frame
None of this means India is a bad place to source from. The opposite — Jaipur and its surrounding clusters are where the craft is, where the price points work, and where the design vocabulary for handcrafted goods is deepest. But sourcing well from India is a skill, built through hundreds of orders and relationships. We&apos;ve built that skill so you don&apos;t have to learn it the hard way.

Q: What categories do you source?
Jewellery (silver, brass, gold-plated, stone-set), handicrafts and décor, furniture, home furnishings (block-print textiles, bedding, rugs), and handcrafted apparel. We focus on categories that Jaipur and surrounding clusters are genuinely good at — handcrafted and artisanal work, not commodity production.
Q: Where are your workshops?
Primarily in Jaipur and the surrounding region — Sanganer (block printing), Bagru (natural-dye textiles), Jodhpur (wooden furniture), Sikar (handicrafts). For specific categories that aren&apos;t strong in Jaipur, we route to Mumbai, Delhi, or other clusters.
Q: What&apos;s the minimum order quantity?
Varies by category. Jewellery: 50-100 pieces per SKU. Textiles: typically 100-200 meters or 50-100 finished pieces per design. Furniture: 5-20 pieces per design depending on complexity. Apparel: 100-300 pieces per design. We confirm exact MOQs based on your specific spec.
Q: Do you mark up workshop prices?
No. You pay the workshop directly at their quoted price. Our fees come from you only — transparent, no hidden margins.
Q: How is this different from Alibaba?
Alibaba lists workshops. We&apos;ve physically visited ours, audited their capabilities, and removed workshops from our network for falling below our standards. You&apos;re paying for vetting and accountability, not search results.
Q: I&apos;m just starting my brand and don&apos;t have orders yet. Can I work with you?
Yes. Many of our clients are early-stage or pre-revenue founders making their first production decisions. Our vendor sourcing engagement is built for exactly this.
Q: What are your payment terms?
Transparent and structured. Our fees: typically a deposit on engagement start and balance on completion. Workshop production costs go directly from you to the workshop at quoted price.
Q: How long until first delivery?
From engagement start to first sample: 2-3 weeks. From sample approval to bulk delivery: typically 4-8 weeks depending on category and volume. Total: roughly 6-11 weeks for a first order.
Q: Do you take commissions from suppliers?
No. Our income is your fees only. Workshop quotes go to you at net price — we audit them but don&apos;t mark them up.
